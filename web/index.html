Severance peaks at the edge of the city, not beyond it

The detour index is how far you must drive to reach a point directly across the road. An index of 40 means forty times the straight-line distance. Measured in concentric rings from the centre, the cost does not simply rise with distance. It climbs steeply to a peak between 26 and 40 kilometres out, then falls away.

A reading, not a conclusion. The fringe is where highway-grade divided roads already exist but the local street network behind them has not filled in, so the severance has nothing to absorb it. Further out there are fewer divided roads and less severance. The shape is consistent with that explanation and has not been tested against it. Patancheru and Isnapur, where this project began, sit at roughly 36 km — inside the peak ring.

Areas with fewer than thirty measured points are computed but excluded, because a median over a handful of points is noise rather than a finding.

Read the ranking, not the numbers. The open map under-records small connections — median openings and service roads drivers use daily are often missing. That inflates every absolute figure here by an unknown amount. It affects all areas alike, so comparing one to another holds, while individual values do not yet.

Where a typical route runs past ten kilometres to cross twenty metres, a hole in the map is the likelier explanation than a real detour, and the row says so. Those are the first places we go back and correct the map.

The city in three dimensionsbeing built

A descent from the globe down to a single junction, with severance carried in height and colour, and a click through to any area's own measurements. It is specified and it is not built, and the reason is worth stating rather than hiding behind a spinner.

What it will show

Real building heights from open satellite-derived data, so a severed block reads as somewhere people live rather than a shape on a plan. Severance encoded in height and colour together, so the worst places are visible in the opening frame without reading a legend.

What it will not have

Terrain. Hyderabad spans 32 m of elevation across the whole measured area, so relief here would be drama invented in a shader. That was tested before it was designed, and the threshold was written before the test ran.

Street-level imagery

Street View is Google's and is never used here, at any point, in any form. The open alternatives were checked on 25 October 2026: Panoramax returned one image across the entire 85 by 77 km area. Mapillary is still being probed.

If neither has real coverage, there will be no imagery layer and this page will say so, rather than shipping a button that works one time in three.

Why it is not here yet. Polish reads as authority. A beautiful model of a number nobody has checked would persuade people of something we do not know, and when it broke it would take the method down with it rather than just the picture. The model comes after a route has been driven.

How it is measured

  1. Take a point on a road modelled as two separate carriageways.
  2. Find the nearest point on the opposite carriageway, checked to be genuinely across the road rather than a flyover overhead or an unrelated street.
  3. Ask the road network for the shortest legal driving route between the two.
  4. Divide driving distance by straight-line distance.

What it does not measure

Travel time

Distance and geometry only. Congestion and roadworks are deliberately excluded — a median still stands when the construction ends, and that is the claim worth making.

Walking

The open map holds 407 pedestrian crossings and 212 footbridges for a metropolitan area of roughly ten million, and none within 400 m of Abids or Secunderabad station. Absence in the map is not absence on the ground, so every claim here is about driving.

Intent or blame

We publish what an authority recorded and what we measured. Never what we think that means about anyone's motives.

Known limitations

Kept current, in public, because a limitation found by a reader is worse than one we published ourselves.

Help, and the questions worth asking

Every answer below cites where it comes from, so you can check it rather than take it. If an answer here disagrees with the data, the data is right and this page is out of date — tell us.

Found a mistake?

That is the most useful thing you can send. Say which area or junction, and what the road actually does. A correction to the underlying map improves every navigation app in the city, not only this one.
