A reading, not a conclusion. On the fringe, highway-grade divided roads already exist but the street network behind them has not filled in, so nothing absorbs the severance. Further out there are fewer divided roads. The shape fits this explanation; it has not been tested against it. Patancheru and Isnapur, where this project began, sit at roughly 36 km — inside the peak ring.

"Beyond ORR" here means everything past 26 km, including points up to 85 km from the centre. On 26 October 2026 we found that 12.2% of the measured set lay outside the study area this project had declared. Those points score lower than the true fringe, so every ratio in this table understates the fringe rather than overstating it. The ring table above is the corrected view. The next run will be clipped to the declared area and these figures replaced, not revised.

Why the numbers run high

The open map under-records small connections — median openings and service roads drivers use daily are often missing. That inflates every absolute figure here by an unknown amount. It affects all areas alike, so comparing one to another holds, while individual values do not yet.

Where a typical route runs past ten kilometres to cross twenty metres, a hole in the map is the likelier explanation than a real detour, and the row says so. Those are the first places we go back and correct the map.

Areas with fewer than thirty measured points are left out: a median over a handful of points is noise.

How it is measured

  1. Take a point on a road modelled as two separate carriageways.
  2. Find the nearest point on the opposite carriageway, checked to be genuinely across the road rather than a flyover overhead or an unrelated street.
  3. Ask the road network for the shortest legal driving route between the two.
  4. Divide driving distance by straight-line distance.

What it leaves out

Travel time, walking, and anyone's motives.

Why

Travel time

Distance and geometry only. Congestion and roadworks are deliberately excluded — a median still stands when the construction ends, and that is the claim worth making.

Walking

The open map holds 407 pedestrian crossings and 212 footbridges for a metropolitan area of roughly ten million, and none within 400 m of Abids or Secunderabad station. Absence in the map is not absence on the ground, so every claim here is about driving.

Intent or blame

We publish what an authority recorded and what we measured. Never what we think that means about anyone's motives.
