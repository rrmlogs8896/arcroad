U-turns worth a second look.

Some median openings on our divided roads sit just before or just after a junction, or a short distance from another opening. Each one adds a place where vehicles cut across the fast lane or wait in it. We are finding them from open data across the Cyberabad IT corridor, checking a sample on the road, and publishing only what that check confirms.

What we look for

A median opening is a gap in the divider of a divided road where vehicles can turn across. Guidance on access control (IRC:62) wants openings at intersections, and spaced well apart elsewhere. So each opening falls into one of three groups.

Not flaggedAt a junctionWithin 30 m of a crossing road or a signal. The opening is the junction itself, which is where guidance wants it.
Second lookNext to a junction30 to 100 m from one. Close enough to add a second crossing point to the junction's own queue.
Second lookClose to another openingLess than 150 m from the next mid-block opening on the same road, so two gaps do the work of one.

What we will not claim

We will not say that an opening causes a jam or an accident, or that it is unnecessary. Open data has no traffic speeds, vehicle counts or crash records for these points, so it cannot carry that weight. We say which openings are worth a second look, and why, so that the people responsible can look. We make no claim about walking: OpenStreetMap's footpath and crossing data for Hyderabad is not complete enough.

How a result gets published

Ten flagged openings are drawn at random, with the random seed written down before the draw, and checked on the road or in dated photos. If at least seven of the ten are real openings that match the map, the full result is published here with the check. If fewer than seven are, the result is withdrawn and we publish that instead: it would mean the map, not the road, is the problem.

Know an opening like this?

If a median opening near you sits just before a junction or right beside another one, report it. Reports are kept in a separate, labelled layer and never mixed into the measured figures, but they tell us where to look first.
