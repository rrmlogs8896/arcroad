Where a U-turn sits decides how far you drive.

On Cyberabad's signal-free roads, a place just across the divider can be a long drive away. arcroad measures that hidden distance with open data, checks it on the road, and can measure any change before the city makes it.

Who it helps

The same measurement answers a different question for each person on the road.

Buses and office shuttles

Protect public transport

For each bus and shuttle route affected by a change such as the one-way loop, the extra kilometres per trip, from the open timetable data, so a trial does not quietly penalise the people who left their cars.

Residents and commuters

Know the real trip

See how far a crossing really is, by two-wheeler or car, and report a gap in the divider that has opened or closed, for the authorities to check. A public version is planned; it is not live yet.

Cyberabad Traffic Police

Measure before and after

Extra distance for the trips you name, before, during and after a trial, alongside the speed and density you already track. The method and code are open, so the police or SCSC can run it themselves.

Cyberabad Municipal Corporation

Plan the medians

Openings ranked by the extra distance per trip, and by distance × trips once traffic counts are shared, so median and junction works go where they save the most.

For your consideration

Three steps, each low-cost and reversible, alongside the recent U-turn reviews at Khajaguda and Malkam Cheruvu.

1 · Measure the one-way loop trial

For the proposed loop around Biodiversity Junction, IKEA Rotary, Mindspace C-Gate, Knowledge City and Dallas Centre, arcroad returns the extra distance for every trip between those five points, both ways (20 trips), for cars and buses, promptly of receiving the route. Today's baseline is already computed (Modelled).

2 · Review the longest detours together

At places the police and CMC choose, we supply the driven detour and the road layout from open data. Options include a relocated U-turn, a pedestrian crossing or a bus stop on both sides, tried only where CMC and the Traffic Police judge it safe. Distance alone should not decide whether an opening stays.

CMC Engineering designs and approves; Traffic Police operate; arcroad measures

3 · Keep one checked list of authorised openings

Only openings confirmed as authorised are shown. Citizens' reports of new or closed gaps go privately to CMC and the Traffic Police to check, and are never shown as usable crossings. Each opening carries a last-checked date and exports as a standard map file CMC can import.

How we know

Every figure says how it was checked.

Open map data

Distances come from OpenStreetMap, the open world map, cross-checked against two other free direction tools.

Driven on the road

We drive the places the model flags, with GPS, and publish every pass, including the ones that failed.

Written down first

The error margin was fixed before the first drive and has never changed. Every later rule is dated and re-applied to every drive, and we withdrew our own first loop estimate when it failed the check.

Limits we state openly
  • The figures measure distance, not safety. We make no claim that any U-turn causes crashes.
  • IRC:SP:84-2014, the four-laning manual for national highways, sets a minimum of 500 m between median openings in built-up stretches (clause 2.14.1). It is a minimum spacing for highways, cited for reference only; spacing on city roads is decided case by case.
  • Each place has one driver, one car and mostly one pass per direction; phone GPS carries about ±50 m of noise on a 1–3 km loop. Weekday peak times will differ from our drive times.
  • We make no claim about walking: open footpath and crossing data is not yet complete enough.
  • We publish only per-pass summaries of our own team's drives, with our consent. Raw tracks and photos stay private, and any photo shown is blurred for faces and number plates. This page collects no personal data.

What we ask

If Cyberabad Traffic Police share the proposed loop's roads, direction and trial dates, we return the before-and-after distances promptly, to the officer who sent them, and to the Society for Cyberabad Security Council (SCSC) and CMC if the police ask. We publish nothing about the trial, before or after, without written permission from the Traffic Police, and are glad to sign a simple data-use letter in advance.
