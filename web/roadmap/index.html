Plans · open to pilot partners

Where arcroad goes next.

One phase is live today in Hyderabad. The next two are planned and open to city teams who want to try them with us. Nothing here is a promise of dates.

Phase 1 · Live today: the Hyderabad pilot

Every crossing in our sample modelled from open map data, ten driven directions checked on the road, an open cost calculator, a baseline for all 20 trips between the five proposed Hi-Tech City loop points, and a way to report a changed median opening. See the entry · See the drives · Try the calculator

Phase 2 · Planned: the rest of Hyderabad

ForWhat it would doStatus
CitizensA phone-friendly web tool, nothing to install: tap any proposed U-turn or closure and see the extra distance and cost for your own trip. Report a gap, a queue or a dark stretch with a blurred photo.Planned
Traffic and city teamsA separate, signed-in sandbox in the browser: try a closure or a one-way change on a copy of the map before barriers go down, see car, shuttle and bus detours, and export standard GeoJSON and GTFS files for existing control-room tools.Planned · in exploration

Why web first and not an app: commuters already carry navigation apps and teams already run several systems. A web tool needs no install and no app-store review, and works on any phone.

Phase 3 · Planned: Bengaluru, then other Indian cities

The same open method, open map and open bus data, starting with Bengaluru's corridors, then offered as a free toolkit to any city mapped on OpenStreetMap.

A lesson from the Outer Ring Road

The Outer Ring Road avoids the crossing problem by design: it has no at-grade turns, only interchanges, with service roads alongside for local trips. That is why we leave it out of our sample. A city loop cannot be built like an expressway, but two of its ideas carry over where a loop is unavoidable: a service lane for short local hops, and a grade-separated U-turn at the busiest point, so turning traffic does not queue in a through lane. Whether either fits a given junction is for the city's engineers; the loop baseline on the entry page is where that discussion can start.

Every road as clear as the Outer Ring Road

On the Outer Ring Road a driver always knows what comes next: numbered exits, signs well before each one, and the same rules for every lane. Our main junctions and loops rarely give that warning. A driver often learns that a road has turned one-way, or where the next U-turn is, only on reaching it. The aim for the years ahead is that every road, a single carriageway, a divided road or a loop, reads just as clearly.

  • Name and number every junction and U-turn opening on a corridor, the way expressway exits are numbered. Signs, maps, complaints and news reports all point to the same place.
  • Advance signs before every loop or one-way stretch, showing the loop and the distance to the next U-turn. Time to pick a lane, instead of a late swerve.
  • One public map for each loop: direction, entry and exit points, U-turn openings, open for map apps to use. Phone directions that match the road on the day.
  • Lane arrows and markings that agree with the signs, renewed after every resurfacing. No guessing at a faded arrow.
  • A dated public notice and a temporary sign before an opening closes or a loop starts. No surprise detour on a regular trip.
  • The detour measured before and after each change. Clarity judged by distance saved for residents, not only by speed.

These are proposals for the city's engineers to weigh, not findings. arcroad can supply the last one: the open detour measurement.
